# Judgment — oa-W7220097264

- Records: oa-W7220097264
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The doubt recorded at screening was I1. The paper never names its game or headset and never says that body movement drives the game. Amendment A6 now covers this case: an intervention the paper calls an exergame, and describes as exercise performed in the game, meets I1 unless the text shows the input is not body movement. The paper calls the intervention a "VR exergame intervention" and describes exercise inside the game: "Participants engage in basic VR exergame tasks emphasizing coordination, movement accuracy, and responsiveness" (Session 2) and "The fourth session introduces higher-intensity VR exergame tasks that require endurance and focus" (Session 4). Nothing in the text says the input is something other than movement. I1 is met under A6.

The other criteria are met:
- I2: "randomly assigned".
- I3: the control arm had "traditional physical activity without VR".
- I4: "eight structured sessions over two months".
- I5: BREQ-3.
- I6: one composite score is reported per arm at post-intervention and at follow-up, which A7 accepts.
- I7: "Published online 01 April 2025".
- I8: the paper is in English.

## Reasons

**D1 Randomization process: Some concerns.** The only statement is that participants "were randomly assigned". The paper names no method for generating the sequence and says nothing about concealment. That fails the Low anchor and is the anchor's own example of Some concerns. Baseline motivation is similar (2.91 vs 2.87), and nothing shows allocation was predictable, so High does not apply.

**D2 Deviations from intended interventions: Some concerns.** The paper never states which participants were analysed: there is no ITT or per-protocol statement, and the group test's F(1,27) implies 29 analysed rather than 30, with no explanation. An undescribed analysis population is a named Some concerns case. Nothing shows a per-protocol analysis with exclusions or contamination, so the paper does not reach High. The control arm's content is not described at all. The SDT counselling elements belong to the VR programme by design and are not deviations.

**D3 Missing outcome data: Some concerns.** The paper never reports how many were analysed per arm. It has no flow diagram, reports no dropouts and says nothing about missing-data handling. "Numbers analysed not reported" is a named Some concerns case. The degrees of freedom suggest one participant missing, about 3%, so the more-than-20% High threshold is not shown to be met.

**D4 Measurement of the outcome: High.**
- Low is out because participants knew their arm.
- The paper fails the Some concerns anchor. That anchor needs the outcome to be a published, validated instrument given the same way in both arms. The BREQ-3 is published and validated, but the outcome analysed is not the BREQ-3 as described. It is one "Exercise Motivation" score, built from six subscales that run in opposite directions (amotivation through intrinsic regulation), and the paper does not say how it was formed.
- The VR arm's post-intervention mean of 4.08 is above the stated item maximum of 4 ("ranging from 0 ... to 4"). So the reported score cannot be a mean of the instrument as administered.
- A7 sends the formation of a composite to D4. A composite that is undescribed and outside the instrument's range is an unvalidated scoring, which falls under "otherwise unvalidated". Administration in each arm is also not described.
- Between the two anchors I take the more severe.

**D5 Selection of the reported result: High.** The paper gives no registration or protocol, which alone would be Some concerns. But the High anchor applies directly. The Methods say the BREQ-3 has six subscales and that "Higher scores in each subscale indicate stronger endorsement". The report gives none of the six subscales, only one composite. That is a report giving only part of what its methods say was administered. The time points do match the Methods: baseline, post-intervention and five months.
