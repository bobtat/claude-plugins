# Full-text disagreements — second retrieval pass (round oa)

Two screeners judged each record independently from `fulltext/<record_id>.txt` under `screening/brief-fulltext-r2.md`. On the first record they differ on the decision; on the other two both exclude but cite different exclusion codes, and the code is the reason the flow diagram counts.

## `oa-W7220200190` — Impact of Mobile Fitness Game Apps on Motivation and Lifestyle Change Readiness (2025)

- **Screener A:** exclude — E1: Not an exergame
  - Rationale: Intervention: "Each session integrates game-based elements (points, levels, rewards) with behavioral strategies"; activities are "step-count battles, mini-games, or aerobic tasks". The text does not show movement as the game input. This reads as gamification of a fitness app. (Measured with BREQ-2, results by arm given.)
  - Doubt: I1: The authors call it an exergame, but the text never says that body movement drives the game or that games have a score pursued by movement. Where is the line for a gamified fitness app with mini-games and step-count battles? A different reading could include it.
- **Screener B:** include
  - Rationale: Methods: "The questionnaire consists of 19 items divided into five" subscales (BREQ-2 meets I5). Results: "while the control group showed minimal change" with Table 1 giving BREQ motivation means for intervention (2.96 to 3.87) and control (3.01 to 3.12) at post. Randomized 15 vs 15; 15 sessions over three months; control got health education materials; published online 2025-04-01.
  - Doubt: I1: intervention is a "mobile fitness game app" with points/levels/rewards and "step-count battles, mini-games, or aerobic tasks" delivered in facilitated sessions; the paper never says limb or trunk movement is the game input, so it may be gamification rather than an exergame (authors call it exergame). I2: randomization method not described.

## `oa-W4413463698` — Research on the strategy of optimizing the training path of sports dance and aesthetics for college students in general colleges and universities based on mathematical modeling (2025)

- **Screener A:** exclude — E1: Not an exergame
  - Rationale: Section III.A: "the comparison table of the joint coordinates of the dance trainee and the standard movement" database - Kinect used for movement detection and joint-angle feedback; no game with a goal or running score is described (motivation also reported with no named instrument).
  - Doubt: E1: Does Kinect joint-coordinate comparison to a standard movement database count as a movement-training system with a running score (amendment A4)? The text shows no score. E5 would also apply: motivation scores come from an unnamed 'interest in learning sports dance' measure with no instrument name or items.
- **Screener B:** exclude — E5: No named motivation instrument
  - Rationale: Results V.A: "The results of the comparison of the experimental group's interest in learning physical education dance before and" after - an unnamed interest/motivation scale with five dimensions; no instrument is named
  - Doubt: I1: The Kinect-based aesthetic training is described only in the abstract and the experimental arm's session content is not described as a game with a goal or score; E1 may also apply

## `pm-42261097` — Wearable Myoelectric Interface for Neurorehabilitation (MINT) to Recover Arm Activity After Stroke: A Randomized Controlled Trial. (2026)

- **Screener A:** exclude — E3: No non-exergame comparator
  - Rationale: Methods: "(4) sham group, who used one muscle to control a cursor in 1D." Every arm played the MINT game; the IMI was also reported for all participants together, not by arm (Results: "73% of participants who completed the study agreed at least slightly that MINT was enjoyable"). Preprint of 2025; no other report named.
  - Doubt: I1: Is an EMG-controlled cursor game (muscle activation, not limb movement) an exergame? I3: Is the one-muscle sham game a non-exergame comparator or an exergame? Read as a game for this decision. E6 would also apply since IMI results are not given by arm.
- **Screener B:** exclude — E1: Not an exergame
  - Rationale: Methods: "sham group, who used one muscle to control a cursor in 1D" - the MINT game is an EMG-controlled cursor task (muscle activation not limb movement as input). Also IMI was modified and reported pooled: "Mean scores were calculated for all participants, overall survey responses, and within each category." (no results by arm). Text is the medRxiv preprint of the record.
  - Doubt: I1: does an EMG-driven cursor game with a goal count as an exergame (limb or trunk movement as game input)? If yes then every arm (including 1-muscle sham) plays the game so E3 would apply instead, and E6 applies in any case since IMI results are not given by arm.
