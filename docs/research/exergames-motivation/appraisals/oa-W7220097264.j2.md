# Judgment — oa-W7220097264

- Records: oa-W7220097264
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The screening doubt was I1: the paper never names its game or headset and never says that
body movement is the game input. Amendment A6 now decides this. The paper calls the
intervention an exergame and describes exercise done in the game: "Participants engage in
basic VR exergame tasks emphasizing coordination, movement accuracy, and responsiveness"
(Intervention, Session 2), and "The fourth session introduces higher-intensity VR exergame
tasks that require endurance and focus" (Session 4). Nothing in the text shows that the input
is something other than body movement, so A6's exception does not apply and I1 is met.

The other criteria are met. I2: "Participants were randomly assigned" (Study Design and
Participants). I3: the control did "traditional physical activity without VR". I4: "eight
structured sessions over two months". I5: BREQ-3. I6: one composite motivation score is
reported for both arms after baseline (Table 1), which A7 accepts. I7: "Published online 01
April 2025". I8: English. The scoring problems (below) belong to D4 and D5, not to
eligibility.

## Reasons
**D1 Some concerns.** The paper says only that participants were "randomly assigned". It
names no sequence method and says nothing about concealment, so it fails the Low anchor.
Baseline motivation is similar (2.91 against 2.87, Table 1), and nothing shows allocation was
predictable or that randomization failed, so the High anchor is not met.

**D2 Some concerns.** The analysis population is never described: there is no mention of
intention-to-treat or per-protocol analysis. The group test's F(1,27) suggests 29 were
analysed out of 30 randomized, without explanation. The anchor names "an analysis
population that is not described" as Some concerns. No per-protocol-only analysis,
contamination or unbalanced deviation is reported, so the High anchor is not met. The VR
arm's goal-setting and reflection content is part of the planned intervention, not a
deviation from it.

**D3 Some concerns.** The number analysed per arm is not reported, and there is no flow
diagram and no account of losses at post-test or at five months. The anchor names "numbers
analysed not reported" as Some concerns. The degrees of freedom suggest at most one
participant was lost, so nothing shows more than 20% missing or missingness that differs
between arms, and the High anchor is not met.

**D4 High.** The paper names a validated instrument, the BREQ-3. It describes the items as
scored "on a 5-point Likert scale" running from 0 to 4, and scored by subscale:
"Higher scores in each subscale indicate stronger endorsement" (Data Collection).
The outcome analysed, however, is a single Exercise Motivation score per arm (Table 1). The paper never says how this score was formed from six
subscales that point in opposite directions (amotivation and external regulation against
intrinsic regulation). It is not the validated relative autonomy index either, which is
weighted and does not fall in a 0–4 range. The VR arm's post-intervention mean of 4.08 is
above the highest possible item score of 4, which a mean of 0–4 items cannot be. So the
score analysed is not shown to be the validated instrument scored as validated. That fails
the Some concerns condition ("a published, validated one") and meets the High condition
("otherwise unvalidated"). The mode and timing of administration are also not described for
either arm. Low is not attainable.

**D5 Some concerns.** Re-rated on 2026-11-28 under amendment A10. No registration or
protocol is cited anywhere; the Ethical Considerations say only "All ethical principles were
adhered in conducting and writing this article.", so the Low anchor is not met. The Methods
name six BREQ-3 subscales and the report gives only one composite Exercise Motivation score;
under A10 a composite reported in place of subscales the Methods name is Some concerns, not
High, because no registration or protocol named the subscales as outcomes (how the composite
was formed stays under D4). Nothing else meets the High anchor: the three time points the
Methods give (baseline, post-intervention, five months) are all reported for both arms in
Table 1, and there is no registered outcome for the report to differ from.
