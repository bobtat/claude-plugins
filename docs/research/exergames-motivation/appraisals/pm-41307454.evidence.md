## Coding notes
- `exergame_platform` = immersive vr headset. The intervention is a VirZoom stationary bike (exercise equipment with game) connected to a PlayStation VR headset. The protocol codes custom software in a commercial headset as `immersive vr headset`; the paper itself says "immersive VR cycling regimen using the commercial VirZoom VR exercise bike connected to a PlayStation VR system (Sony), which includes a head-mounted display" (Intervention Group, line 80). Choice was between this value and `exercise equipment with game`.
- `trial_scale` = not stated. The paper never calls itself a pilot or feasibility trial; it says "2-arm randomized controlled trial" (line 42).
- `motivation_role` = exploratory or unspecified. Motivation is one of several psychological outcomes ("health-related psychological (ie, motivation, mood, and depressive symptoms)", line 38); no outcome is labelled primary or secondary except "The change in heart rate ... served as the primary outcome for cardiovascular fitness" (line 71).
- `comparator_type` = no intervention or wait-list. Control "maintain their usual routine and daily activities" and the paper says "no-intervention control group" (lines 42, 84).
- `direction` = favours exergame. No composite score is reported. Identified regulation and intrinsic regulation (both with valence) have interaction P=.02 in Table 2 and favour the exergame arm; amotivation (P=.05 in Table 2, text reports only a main effect of time), external and introjected regulation show no interaction reported as significant. No subscale favours the comparator.
- `setting` = laboratory or university: "the intervention was delivered in a supervised, in-person laboratory setting" (line 281).
- `population_group` = adults: inclusion "college students aged between 18 and 35 years" (line 46).
- `n_randomized`: the paper gives 38 randomized in total and 17 + 19 analysed; it does not state the per-arm numbers randomized.

## Domain evidence

### D1 Randomization process
- Sequence: "Randomization was executed using a computer-generated sequence in Microsoft Excel, allocating participants in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention or control group." (Randomization, line 77)
- Concealment: no statement of allocation concealment, who generated the sequence, who enrolled participants, or who assigned them. Searched Methods (Study Design, Procedure, Randomization), Participant Flow, Limitations. The paper states "Following baseline assessments, participants were randomized into the intervention or control group." (Procedure, line 74)
- Block size or stratification: not stated.
- Baseline balance: "The chi-square test and the independent t test indicated that there were no statistically significant differences in demographic, physiological, and psychological characteristics between the intervention and control groups at baseline." (line 98)
- Baseline BREQ-2, Table 1 (intervention vs control): "Amotivation" "1.57 (0.64)" "1.75 (0.64)" ".41"; "External regulation" "1.94 (0.71)" "2.32 (0.86)" ".17"; "Introjected regulation" "3.33 (1.14)" "2.86 (0.88)" ".17"; "Identified regulation" "4.00 (0.71)" "3.74 (0.65)" ".25"; "Intrinsic regulation" "3.71 (0.94)" "3.63 (0.86)" ".81".
- Age at baseline: "Age (y), mean (SD) 22.82 (2.51) 24.84 (4.14) .09".
- Timing: recruitment and data collection "between August 2020 and March 2021" (line 45); baseline assessment preceded randomization.
- The Participant Flow text reports "2 (5%) participants dropped out of the intervention group during the first week" after randomization (line 94).

### D2 Deviations from intended interventions
- Participants and personnel: the control group was "instructed to maintain their usual routine and daily activities, without engaging in any other PA interventions" and "We did not formally measure the PA levels of the control group throughout the study." (Control Group, line 84)
- Limitations: "we did not objectively track the control group's PA during the intervention period, which may limit the certainty that they maintained usual routines" (line 281; the text renders the apostrophe as a curly one).
- Adherence to the intervention: "tracking attendance and adherence through exercise session logs" (line 87); no attendance or adherence figures are reported anywhere (searched Results, Tables 1-4, Discussion).
- Analysis population: "Finally, 36 participants (intervention group: n=17, 47%; control group: n=19, 53%) completed baseline and postintervention assessments. Their data were entered for statistical analysis." (line 94). No statement of intention-to-treat or per-protocol analysis. The two post-randomization dropouts were not analysed.
- Outliers: "A robust regression-based outlier detection procedure was used to identify outliers, and any identified outliers were excluded from the analyses." (line 90). Number of outliers excluded and which outcomes: not stated.
- Co-intervention and context: "this study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic and adopted a no-intervention control arm" (line 281); "Hawthorne effect" and attention effects "cannot be ruled out" (line 281).
- Contamination (control using exergames): not mentioned. Searched Control Group, Limitations.
- Not a crossover trial.

### D3 Missing outcome data

Notably, 2 (5%) participants dropped out of the intervention group during the first week of intervention, expressing concerns about participating in an in-person study during the COVID-19 pandemic." (line 94)
- Analysed: intervention n=17, control n=19 (line 94; Table 1 headings "Intervention (n=17)" "Control (n=19)"). Numbers randomized per arm are not stated; the flow text implies 19 per arm (17 + 2), but this is an inference. Figure 2 (CONSORT) is not in the saved text.

- Outlier exclusion (line 90) is not reconciled with the stated analysed n of 36 and df "F 1,34" (line 155): no outlier count is given; "Our final analyzed sample of 36 exceeded this requirement." (line 45)
- Missing item-level data and handling (imputation): not described.
- Pre-set sample size: "the required total sample size was 34" (line 45).

### D4 Measurement of the outcome
- Instrument: "Participants' general exercise motivation was assessed by the Behavioral Regulation in Exercise Questionnaire-2 (BREQ-2) [ 27 ]. The BREQ-2 has 19 items assessing the continuum of behavioral regulation in exercise, which includes amotivation, external regulation, introjected regulation, identified regulation, and intrinsic regulation. The BREQ-2 has demonstrated good reliability and validity in previous research." (line 62; apostrophe in "Participants'" is curly in the saved text)
- No modification of items or referent is described; searched Measurements, Procedure, Limitations. Reliability in this sample (e.g., Cronbach alpha): not reported.
- Administration: "After 4 weeks, all participants returned for postintervention assessments identical to the baseline measures." (line 74); same at baseline in both arms; "Both groups received identical assessments at baseline and after intervention." (line 84). Mode of administration (paper, online) and who administered: not stated.
- Instrument referent is "general exercise motivation", the same wording for both arms.
- Blinding: no statement that participants, or outcome assessors, were blind to arm or hypothesis. The Introduction states the hypothesis of improvement in the intervention group (line 38); whether participants were told it is not stated.
- Limitation noted by authors: "the control group did not engage in an active exercise condition, so the contribution of nonspecific effects, such as attention or novelty (Hawthorne effect), cannot be ruled out." (line 281)
- Time points: baseline and 4 weeks only.

### D5 Selection of the reported result
- Registration: "This trial was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov ( NCT06902727 )." (line 49); Abstract: "ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06902727". The paper gives no registration date, and does not say what outcomes or instruments the registration names. The paper does not state that the trial was registered prospectively. Recruitment dates: "between August 2020 and March 2021" (line 45).
- No separate protocol or statistical analysis plan is cited. Searched Methods, Ethical Considerations, Data Availability, References.
- Reported subscales: the Statistical Analysis lists "exercise motivation (amotivation, external regulation, introjected regulation, identified regulation, and intrinsic regulation)" (line 90); Table 2 gives all five subscales at baseline and 4 weeks. No total or relative autonomy index is reported.
- Time points: only baseline and 4 weeks are described in methods; both reported.
- Statistics reported for the interaction: text reports F, P and eta squared only for the two significant interactions ("identified regulation ( F 1,34 =6.55; P =.02; η²=0.16) and intrinsic regulation ( F 1,34 =11.21; P =.02; η²=0.25)", line 155); for the other three subscales only the Table 2 P values are given.
- Multiple outcomes (motivation, five mood subscales, depression, body fat, fitness) analysed without a stated adjustment for multiplicity (searched Statistical Analysis).
- Analysis choice: a 2-way repeated measures ANOVA; the paper describes post hoc analyses without naming the test or adjustment (line 155).

## Inside the paper
- Intrinsic regulation interaction: "( F 1,34 =11.21; P =.02; η²=0.25)" (line 155) and Abstract "intrinsic motivation ( P =.02; η 2 =0.25)". An F of 11.21 on 1 and 34 degrees of freedom does not correspond to P=.02 by the standard F distribution, so the F and the P as printed disagree with each other (my computation, not a statement in the paper). Table 2 gives P ".02" for intrinsic regulation.
- Identified regulation: the paper gives identical statistics for the main effect of time and for the interaction: "identified regulation ( F 1,34 =6.55; P =.02; η²=0.16)" appears in the main-effect list and again in the interaction list (line 155).
- Baseline values differ between Table 1 and the baseline columns of Tables 3 and 4 for the control group: Table 1 "Body fat percentage" "27.65 (5.18)" against Table 4 "Control" "27.28 (5.09)"; Table 1 "BMI (kg/m 2 ), mean (SD)" "25.37 (3.69)" against Table 4 "25.52 (3.71)"; Table 1 "Cardiovascular fitness (bpm), mean (SD)" "115.95 (16.64)" against Table 4 "115.19 (16.64)"; Table 1 "Anger" "1.60 (1.09)" against Table 3 "1.59 (1.09)". Intervention-arm baselines match across tables. The paper does not explain this.
- Amotivation: Table 2 gives P ".05" for amotivation; the text says only that there was "significant main effect of time on amotivation" and lists interactions for identified and intrinsic regulation only.
- Depression score: "In the intervention group, the mean total depression score decreased ... ( P =.03). In contrast, in the control group, the mean total depression score increased ... ( P =.03)." (line 241): the same P is given for the two within-group changes and for the interaction ("P =.03" in the Abstract).
- The Discussion says "These qualitative insights parallel the high adherence and enjoyment reported in our study" (line 276). No adherence data and no enjoyment measure are reported in the Results (searched Results, Tables 1-4).
- The Discussion says "The sustained improvements in motivation and mood" (line 275), while the only post-baseline time point is at the end of the 4-week intervention and "follow-up" is listed as future work (line 287).
- The Abstract reports "Significant time×group interactions indicated enhanced intrinsic motivation"; Table 2 and text also show identified regulation as significant, which the Abstract omits.
- The Discussion states "relatedness through simulated social features, such as" ghost riders, and elsewhere "this mechanism was not directly measured in this study" (line 275).
- Ethics: "approved by the institutional review board of the University of Minnesota Twin Cities" (line 49) while participants were "recruited from a single large public university in the Midwestern United States" (line 45); the paper does not name the recruiting university.
- The Conclusions say the control group showed "pandemic-era control deterioration" and the Limitations say "the observed time×group interactions were likely inflated" (line 281); the Abstract and Results do not carry this qualification.
