## Coding notes
- `design` = `parallel rct`: the paper calls the study "quasi-experimental" and describes allocation as "a pseudo-randomized procedure constrained to produce approximately equal group sizes" (line 45); individual participants, three parallel arms. Amendment A3 counts this as random allocation, so no other list value fits better. The weakness is for D1/D2, not coded here.
- `trial_scale` = `not stated`: the paper does not call itself a pilot or feasibility study (searched Abstract, Study Design, Limitations). It does say "No a priori power analysis was conducted" (line 75).
- `population_group` = `adults`: no inclusion ages are given; the only description is "Thirty-three healthy NCAA Division I intercollegiate student-athletes" (line 45) with "19.8 ± 0.9 years" (line 20). The paper's description decides because no bound is given.
- `health_status` = `healthy or general population`: "Thirty healthy collegiate athletes" (line 20). Prior lower-limb injury was not an exclusion ("individuals with a history of lower extremity injury were not excluded", line 78).
- `setting` = `not stated`: the paper never says where the training sessions took place. Testing used a force plate and Vicon system (line 55) and sessions were "supervised by the primary investigator and a designated research assistant" (line 67), but no venue is named.
- `exergame_platform` = `console or camera motion game`: "The gamified group trained using the Wii Fit Plus system (Nintendo, Redmond, WA)" (line 71), a commercial console title.
- `comparator_type` = `conventional exercise; no intervention or wait-list`: the wobble-board group ("traditional") did standard balance exercises (line 72); the control "did not take part in any study training sessions" (line 70). The control was not "wait-list" as such (no later offer of training is stated), but `no intervention or wait-list` is the nearest list value. Only the wobble-board arm has IMI results (A8).
- `motivation_instrument` = `imi`: "Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI)" (line 64).
- `motivation_role` = `exploratory or unspecified`: the paper does not label any outcome primary or secondary. The stated purpose is "dynamic balance and intrinsic motivation" (line 38) and motivation has its own hypothesis, but the word primary is applied only to the balance measures ("the primary outcomes were objective (i.e., mSEBT reach distances ...", line 155). The choice was between this value and `secondary`; the paper gives no role to motivation, so `exploratory or unspecified`.
- `direction` = `favours exergame`: the authors' one-way ANOVA between gamified and traditional groups, Interest/Enjoyment, "F = 9.4, p <0.01, d =1.50", gamified higher; Perceived Competence, Effort/Importance and Value/Usefulness not significant (line 126, Table 2). Under the protocol rule, one valenced subscale favouring an arm with the rest null takes that arm's value. No test against the no-training control exists because it did not complete the IMI. The choice was between this and `no between-group difference`; the rule decides.
- `country` = `not reported`: authors' affiliations are Ohio University, Georgia Southern University and University of Delaware (line 4), but the paper does not state where the trial was run; "the University's IRB" (line 45) is not named. Not inferred.
- `funding` = `not reported`: no funding statement was found; the only statement is the conflict-of-interest declaration (line 160).

## Domain evidence

### D1 Randomization process
Raising concern:
- Allocation method: "Participants were allocated to the gamified, traditional, or control group using a pseudo-randomized procedure constrained to produce approximately equal group sizes." (line 45; also Abstract line 20 "pseudo-randomized")
- Design label: "This controlled, quasi-experimental study compared the effects of two balance training interventions and a control condition" (line 41-42); "following the TREND statement for non-randomized designs" (line 45).
- Concealment: "Because a single investigator enrolled participants, generated the allocation, and performed all testing, allocation was not concealed, and outcome assessment was not blinded." (line 45); "The primary investigator enrolled participants, randomized group assignments, and conducted all baseline and post-intervention testing." (line 67)
- Sample: "recruited as a convenience sample through an existing athlete wellness program" (line 45).
- Sequence generation method (die, computer, list, minimisation) is not named for allocation; the only die mentioned is for test order: "the testing order was randomized using a traditional die" (line 49). Searched Participants, Procedures, Statistical Analysis, Limitations.
- Baseline motivation cannot be compared: the IMI was given "post-testing" only (line 64).
- Baseline balance: Table 1 gives change scores only; no baseline table of participant characteristics by arm was found in the saved text (searched Results, Tables 1-2). Overall characteristics only: "male: n = 5; female: n = 25; age: 19.8 ± 0.9 years; height: 1.72 ± 0.06 m; mass: 71.0 ± 11.6 kg" (line 78).
- Inconsistent wording on assignment: Participants "were randomly assigned" (line 45) in the same paragraph as "pseudo-randomized".
Answering concern: none found beyond the constraint to equal group sizes being stated.

### D2 Deviations from intended interventions
- Participants and personnel: sessions "supervised by the primary investigator and a designated research assistant" (line 67); the same investigator enrolled, allocated, tested and supervised (line 67).
- Analysis population: "Thirty participants completed the study and were included in the final analyses" (line 78); "Three were excluded due to sport-related injuries that limited their ability to fully participate in athletic activity during the study period, consistent with the predefined exclusion criterion." (line 78); "The three participants excluded for sport-related injury discontinued before completing the protocol and were not included in the final analysis" (line 67). The analysis is completers only; no intention-to-treat analysis is described. Searched Statistical Analysis (line 75).
- Co-interventions: "all student-athletes continued organized team activities (e.g., practices, workouts) during the study" (line 155); control "continued their usual team practices and conditioning and were instructed not to begin any additional balance training" (line 70). Whether any control or wobble-board participant used exergames or other balance training is not reported.
- Fidelity: "all 20 training group participants included in the analysis (gamified: n= 9; traditional: n=11) completed all 12 scheduled sessions (100% attendance among completers)" (line 67).
- Instruction difference: wobble-board participants "were instructed to focus on maintaining consistent engagement with each task, rather than maximizing performance or accuracy" (line 72); no matching instruction is given for the Wii group.
- Not a crossover trial.

### D3 Missing outcome data
- Randomized/recruited: "Thirty-three ... recruited ... gamified training (n = 12), traditional training (n = 11), or control (n = 10)" (line 45).
- Analysed: "gamified: n = 9; traditional: n = 11; control: n = 10" (line 78); Abstract "gamified training (n = 9), traditional training (n = 11), or a control group (n = 10)" (line 20). Taken together the 3 exclusions all come from the gamified arm (12 to 9), but the paper does not say which arm they were in; Figure 1 (which would) is not in the saved text.
- Reason: sport-related injury, "consistent with the predefined exclusion criterion" (line 78). Not attributed to the intervention.
- IMI denominators: Table 2 headers "Gamified N=12" and "Traditional N=11" (lines 128-132), against the text's analysed n=9 for gamified. The number of IMI forms analysed per arm is not unambiguously stated.
- The IMI was given only to the two training groups: "Following post-testing, participants in the two training groups (gamified and traditional) completed the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI)" (line 64). No IMI data exist for the control arm (n=10).
- No handling of missing data (imputation, sensitivity analysis) is described.

### D4 Measurement of the outcome
- Instrument and validation: "The IMI has acceptable internal consistency (α≥0.70) and strong construct validity (0.70 to 0.91). 23" (line 64); reference 23 is Ryan & Deci 2000 (line 163). Version and items: "Four subscales relevant to the study aims were assessed: Interest/Enjoyment, Perceived Competence, Effort/Importance, and Value/Usefulness." (line 64); "The 25-item IMI uses a 7-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree)." (line 64). The items are in "Supplementary Table XX" (not in saved text); wording and referent adaptation cannot be checked.
- Timing and mode: given once, after post-testing, to training groups only (line 64). "They were informed that all responses were confidential and instructed to answer honestly." (line 64). Who administered the IMI is not stated; the primary investigator conducted post-testing (line 67).
- Hypothesis awareness: participants' knowledge of the hypothesis is not described. The orientation: "all participants were briefed on the benefits of balance training" (line 150).
- Items asking about the game: the paper does not say whether items refer to "the game" or "the wobble board"; not stated.
- Subscale selection: four subscales reported; "Pressure/Tension" is not mentioned (the 25-item version quoted is stated, with four subscales).
- Scoring note: "with higher scores indicating better performance" (line 64).
- Assessor blinding for the balance outcomes: "outcome assessment was not blinded" (line 45).

- Registration: none found. Searched Abstract, Methods, Conflicts of Interest, Limitations; no registry or protocol mention. The paper says it is "reported in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of Evaluations with Nonrandomized Designs (TREND) statement" (line 42).
- Pre-specified hypothesis: "It was further hypothesized that participants in the gamified training group would report higher levels of intrinsic motivation than the traditional training group." (line 38); "Intrinsic motivation, including interest/enjoyment, perceived competence, and value/usefulness, was measured using the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory." (line 38). Effort/Importance is not in the Introduction's list but is in Methods and Results.
- Reported against administered: Methods list four subscales (line 64); Results and Table 2 give all four (lines 126-141).
- Analyses: "Separate one-way ANOVAs were performed to compare Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI) subscale scores ... between the gamified and traditional training groups." (line 75). The Abstract states "IMI scores were compared between training groups using one-way ANOVAs." (line 20).
- No multiplicity correction for four subscale tests is mentioned (searched Statistical Analysis).
- Time points: one, after training.

Table 1 gives "Control N=11" (line 87) against "control: n = 10" (line 78). The Participants section says gamified n = 12 at allocation (line 45). Table 2's N=12 equals the allocated number, not the analysed number.
- Anterior reach: the Abstract says "(gamified: +6.5 ± 10.2 cm; traditional: +6.5 ± 10.3 cm)" (line 23), whereas Table 1 gives anterior reach "5.5±6.2" (gamified) and "6.7±2.6" (traditional) (lines 95-96). The abstract's anterior-reach figures do not appear in Table 1. "6.5±10.3" appears in Table 1 as the traditional group's posterolateral reach (line 111).
- Abstract: "Both training groups showed significant improvements in anterior reach" (line 23); Results: "The traditional group demonstrated greater anterior reach improvements than the control group ( p = 0.034)" and Table 1 post hoc "Traditional > Control" only (lines 80, 97).
- Effect sizes: text gives composite "gamified (p=0.001, d=1.84) and traditional groups (p=0.003, d=1.54)" (line 80); Table 1 gives 2.12 and 1.63 (lines 120-121). Gamified vs traditional "d=0.010" (line 80) against 0.23 in Table 1 (line 122).
- Table 1 anterior reach row: the effect sizes "1.75 [-0.55,1.04]" and "0.25 [0.75,2.70]" (lines 99-101) have confidence intervals that do not contain their own point estimates; the text gives "d=1.61" for the anterior-reach F test (line 80).
- IMI p value: Results text "F = 9.4, p <0.01" (line 126) and the Abstract "p < 0.01" (line 23); Table 2 "< .001*" with footnote "*Indicates significance at p<0.01 level" (lines 134, 142).
- Statistical method: Abstract "Group x time differences were analyzed using repeated measures ANOVA" (line 20); Statistical Analysis "A one-way ANOVA compared pre-to-post changes in mSEBT and DPSI" (line 75).
- Allocation: "Participants were randomly assigned" and "pseudo-randomized procedure" in the same paragraph (line 45); Study Design "quasi-experimental" (line 42).
- Table 2 "Interest/Enjoyment 5.04 ± 1.18 3.17 ± 1.31" matches the Abstract's "(5.0 ± 1.2)" and "(3.2 ± 1.3)" (line 23).
- Discussion: "suggesting that gamified systems may improve adherence" (line 146); adherence was 100% among completers in both training arms (line 67), so no adherence difference was observed.
- The Discussion refers to "the large motivational advantage of the gamified condition" (line 149) while only one of four IMI subscales differed (line 126).
- The paper says IMI was a post-training-only measure, so the Hypothesis that gamified participants "would report higher levels of intrinsic motivation" has no baseline or control-group comparison.
