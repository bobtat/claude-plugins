# Judgment — pm-41628428

- Records: pm-41628428
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The doubt screening raised was I2: the paper calls itself "quasi-experimental", and after random allocation "about 9%-10% (OCTT×2 [n=3] and EBTT+OCTT [n=4]) of participants in each group requested to change their assigned allocation, and these requests were accommodated" (Methods, Study and Trial Designs). The same passage says "These requests were made before commencement of the experimental procedure". That is the case amendment A3 places inside I2: random allocation followed by reassignment of a minority at their own request before the intervention began. So I2 is met as amended, and the weakness is rated under D1/D2.

The other criteria are plainly met. I1: Virtual Tennis on "the PlayStation 3 Move", a motion-controlled console game. I3: the comparator "practiced only on the traditional tennis court", with no exergame. The exergame arm's mixed programme is in under I1. I4: "Both groups performed the task for 12 weeks (24 practice sessions)". I5: the Sport Motivation Scale / "Sports Motivation Test" by Pelletier et al, 7 subscales of 4 items each, which is on I5's In list. I6: Table 3 gives pre and post means for both arms, with between-group P values. I7: published online 02.02.2026, with data collected January to April 2024, and no earlier results report is cited. I8: English.

## Reasons

**D1 Randomization process: Some concerns.** The paper says only "balanced randomization in a ratio of 1:1". It names no sequence-generation method and describes no allocation concealment, so the Low anchor fails. High would need either exploitable non-concealment at assignment or baseline differences pointing to a failed randomization. Neither is shown. Baseline motivation in Table 3 is close between arms (IM-to-know 24.18 vs 24.21; EM-external 24.54 vs 24.34). The largest gap is IM-to-accomplish, 23.29 vs 24.38, about 0.4 SD. Demographics did not differ (Table 1). The preference switches happened after assignment, so I rate them under D2 and not as a failure of concealment before assignment.

**D2 Deviations from intended interventions: Some concerns.** Seven participants changed arm at their own request. The results in Tables 2-3 come from an analysis where "participants were analyzed according to the group they actually participated in, instead of their original randomized groups". The Low anchor therefore fails for the result shown. The paper also ran a modified ITT analysis: completers, analysed in their originally assigned groups, which is the form the Low anchor allows. It reports those results in Multimedia Appendix 1 and says they were "generally consistent". So the High anchor ("per-protocol or as-treated analysis only") is not met as worded, and I take Some concerns. This rating is close to High. The appendix is not in the saved text, so the consistency claim cannot be checked, and the authors themselves say the switching "could potentially affect the internal validity of the study’s results, especially for outcomes related to motivation". No contamination or co-intervention is reported.

**D3 Missing outcome data: Some concerns.** The dropouts were 5/33 (15%) in the exergame arm and 4/33 (12%) in the comparator, and their data "were not used in the analysis". No imputation or sensitivity analysis was done. Both arms fall in the 10-20% band, and the gap between arms is about 3 percentage points, so High (>20%, or a >10-point gap) is not met. The reasons given ("work schedules, health issues, transportation difficulties, or family emergencies") are pooled rather than given per arm, and none is attributed to the intervention. With more than 10% missing in each arm, Low fails.

**D4 Measurement of the outcome: Some concerns.** Participants knew their arm and self-reported, so Low cannot be reached. The instrument is the published Sport Motivation Scale (Pelletier), and its items refer to "their motivation for engaging in sports" in both arms. It was given "pre- and post-12 weeks of training" to both groups. The paper describes no added, removed or rewritten items, no game-specific items, and no difference in how it was administered between arms. No High condition is shown.

**D5 Selection of the reported result: Some concerns.** The paper says "This trial was not preregistered" and cites no protocol, so Low fails. High is not met. All 7 subscales named in the Methods are reported at both time points in Table 3, and only pre and post measurements were planned.
