# Judgment — pm-40567995

- Records: pm-40567995
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
I1 is met under amendment A4: "If a participant's limb angles were within 5° of the standards, they earned points" and "The awarding score would display on the screen as an incentive for the participants" (§2.4). Limb movement is the input and a running score is shown. I2: "Block randomization with a block size of 4 was used" (§2.3). I3: the control "used a video that only presented a professional aerobic dance practitioner's movement but did not show the skeletal simulation. There was also no performance score for feedback as incentives" (§2.4), so it is not an exergame. I4: "sessions were held three times a week for 8 weeks" (§2.4). I5: BREQ-2, a published instrument used in a Chinese translation (§2.5.4). I6 was the screening doubt, raised because no between-group test is clearly labelled. Table 2 reports BREQ-2 means for both arms at weeks 8 and 12 ("EG 47.57 ± 6.75 49.87 ± 8.62 51.27 ± 5.56", "CG 53.97 ± 17.22 55.97 ± 14.77 57.67 ± 14.09"). I6 accepts means, so it is met however the P columns are read. I7: published in 2025 (Front Public Health, 10.3389/fpubh.2025.1498606). I8: English.

## Reasons

**D1 Some concerns.** The paper names a sequence method: computer-generated, blocks of 4, allocation made by independent researchers. It does not say that allocation was concealed until assignment, so it fails Low. The baseline BREQ-2 means differ (47.57 ± 6.75 vs 53.97 ± 17.22, about half a pooled SD), and no test of that difference is shown. The other baseline characteristics in Table 1 are balanced. With n = 30 per arm and an SD of 17 in the control arm, that gap does not on its own point to a failed randomization. The paper also says both "Sixty participants enrolled" (Abstract) and "65 ... randomly assigned" (§3.1). None of this meets the High anchor (predictable or exploitable allocation).

**D2 Some concerns.** The analysis set is the 60 who "finished the training session and all the measurements" out of 65 randomized. No intention-to-treat or per-protocol analysis is named, and the paper never says from which arm the five came. That is an analysis population not described, which the protocol places under Some concerns. Exclusions of 5 of 65 are not shown to be ones that could affect the result, and no contamination or unbalanced co-intervention is reported, so it does not reach High. The different exercise settings (§5) are part of the design, not a deviation.

**D3 Some concerns.** 60 of 65 (92%) were analysed overall. The split by arm and the reasons for loss are not reported, so Low (90% or more in each arm, with no missingness attributed to the intervention) cannot be shown. High cannot be shown either: if all five were lost from one arm of 35, that arm would still have 86% of its data. Numbers analysed per randomized arm are not reported, which is the Some concerns anchor.

**D4 Some concerns.** BREQ-2 is a published instrument, used in a validated Chinese translation (ref 22). It was self-completed "before the walking test", at the same three time points in both arms, and no item changes are reported. That meets the Some concerns conditions. Low is out of reach: participants are called "blinded", but they could see whether or not they had a scored skeleton display, and nothing says they did not know the hypothesis. How the single BREQ-2 score was formed is not stated. That is not one of the High conditions (items altered, game-specific items, different administration, unvalidated instrument), so I did not raise the rating for it.

**D5 Some concerns.** The trial is registered (NCT06216236), but the paper gives no registration date and does not say which outcomes or time points were registered. That is the Some concerns anchor. I considered High: §2.5.4 lists five BREQ-2 aspects and only one total score is reported. But that total spans the whole instrument; it is not a subset of subscales picked from those administered. All three administered time points are reported. So the paper does not meet High's "only some of the subscales or time points" anchor.

## Direction re-examined, 2026-11-28 (amendment A11)
The only BREQ-2 result is a single score per arm ("EG 47.57 ± 6.75 49.87 ± 8.62 51.27 ± 5.56 0.180 0.008 0.936 1.000", Table 2), which is a composite of an instrument whose subscales run in opposite directions ("internal regulation aspect, identity regulation aspect, interjective regulation aspect, external regulation aspect, and motivation", §2.5.4). The paper never says how that score is formed, whether as a sum, a mean or a relative autonomy index, so its valence does not follow from anything stated. Under A11(a) the composite cannot decide `direction`, and the code is `other`, with the between-group P values (0.936, 1.000) left in `motivation_result`. I raised a query suggesting `other` in place of `no between-group difference`.
