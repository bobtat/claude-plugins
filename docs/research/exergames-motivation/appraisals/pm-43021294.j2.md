# Judgment — pm-43021294

- Records: pm-43021294
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The doubt recorded at screening was on I1 (amendment A4 asks the judges to rule again). Appendix 2 settles it: in clinic the player "shifted their body weight while maintaining the prescribed posture to move the on-screen balance board and guide a ball toward the target locations", with "Real-time visual feedback, target achievement, score, and task duration"; at home the player "moved an on-screen avatar using predefined unilateral or bilateral upper-extremity and body positions to avoid obstacles and increase their score". Movement of the trunk and limbs is the input and there is a goal and a score, so I1 is met without needing A4. The arms otherwise share bracing and PSSE, and "the CG continued with conventional exercises", so I3 is met: a mixed programme against conventional exercise. Game use runs weeks 5 to 24 of a 24-week programme (I4). The MSPPA is named as "a validated 16-item, 5-point Likert-type scale ... that measures motivation to participate in physical activity" (I5), with results for both arms in Table 4 (I6). Allocation used sealed envelopes and a randomizer.com sequence (I2). Published 2026 with recruitment from October 2024 (I7). English (I8). Clear.

## Reasons
**D1 Some concerns.** A sequence method is named ("www.randomizer.com" by an independent staff member) and allocation was concealed in "sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes" opened after the baseline assessment. That meets the first two parts of Low. It fails the third: baseline motivation is not similar (33.70 vs 27.84 on MSPPA, effect size 0.72, Table 4), although p = 0.12. With 10 per arm analysed, an imbalance this size is plausible by chance, and nothing else (12 vs 13 under simple randomization, no other baseline differences) points to a failed randomization, so the High anchor is not met.

**D2 High.** The analysis is per-protocol only. Participants who "attended fewer than 80% of the prescribed exercise sessions", or who did not comply with the brace, were excluded after randomization (5 of 25). Exercise adherence is closely tied to the motivation outcome, so these exclusions could affect the result. That meets the High anchor ("Per-protocol or as-treated analysis only, with exclusions or switching that could affect the result"), and the authors themselves say the approach "may overestimate treatment efficacy".

**D3 High.** In the exergame arm 3 of 13 randomized are missing (23.1%, over the 20% threshold); in the control arm 2 of 12 (16.7%). "No imputation method was applied for missing data", and no sensitivity analysis is reported. More than 20% missing in one arm, with no analysis shown to be robust, meets High. The control-arm losses were for "insufficient compliance with brace and exercise treatment", which also bears on motivation.

**D4 Some concerns.** Low cannot be reached: participants knew their arm, and the authors note that this "may have influenced responses to self-reported measures such as the WRVAS, MSPPA". The MSPPA is a published, validated instrument (ref. 14). It was given at the same time points, T0, T1 and T2, in both arms. No item changes or game-specific items are described, and no difference in administration is reported. That meets Some concerns, and nothing under High applies. Who administered it and in what mode is not stated. The paper also does not say how the amotivation subscale enters the 16–80 total. Neither of these meets a High condition.

**D5 Some concerns.** A registration exists (NCT06469021, record date 16/06/2024) and is dated before recruitment began on 14 October 2024. Its contents are not in the saved text, so the paper cannot be shown to match a pre-specified instrument and time point, and Low is not met. I did not rate High. The Methods describe the MSPPA's three subscales, but they do not say subscale scores were computed or analysed. The total is the instrument's defined score ("scored from 16 to 80"). All three between-group analyses of the total are reported, including the non-significant absolute-score tests, although the Abstract foregrounds the "additional ANCOVA". No registered outcome or time point is shown to differ from the one reported. Reporting the total alone and presenting the ANCOVA as an addition are reasons for concern, not demonstrated selection.
