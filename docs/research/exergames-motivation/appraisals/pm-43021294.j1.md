# Judgment — pm-43021294

- Records: pm-43021294
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
Screening's doubt was on I1. Under amendment A4 and I1's own wording, the full paper and Appendix 2 settle it. In the clinic games the input is body movement: "Participants shifted their body weight while maintaining the prescribed posture to move the on-screen balance board and guide a ball toward the target locations", with "Real-time visual feedback, target achievement, score, and task duration" (Appendix 2, Balance Maze). In the home games "Participants moved an on-screen avatar using predefined unilateral or bilateral upper-extremity and body positions to avoid obstacles and increase their score" (Appendix 2, Runner). Trunk and limb movement drives a game that keeps a score, so I1 is met.

The other criteria are met too.
- I2: "the random allocation sequence was created using  www.randomizer.com" (Methods).
- I3: the comparator is conventional exercise, "the CG continued with conventional exercises" (Methods, Interventions). The game arm's programme is mixed, which I1 allows.
- I4: "The intervention period was 24 weeks in both groups", with games from week 5, which still leaves 20 weeks.
- I5: the MSPPA is a named 16-item validated motivation scale.
- I6: totals are given per arm at T1 and T2, and amendment A7 counts a total.
- I7: received 2026.
- I8: the paper is in English.

## Reasons

**D1 Randomization process: Some concerns.** The sequence method is named: it was made on www.randomizer.com by an independent staff member. Allocation was concealed with "sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes" opened after the baseline assessment. That covers two of the three parts of the Low anchor. The third part, similar baseline motivation, fails. Table 4 gives a T0 MSPPA of 33.70 against 27.84, an effect size of 0.72. That baseline is also reported only for the 10 completers per arm, not for everyone randomized. With 10 per arm and p = 0.12 the gap is not evidence that randomization failed, so the High anchor is not met.

**D2 Deviations from intended interventions: High.** Only a per-protocol analysis is reported. It drops participants for non-adherence after randomization, and anyone who "attended fewer than 80% of the prescribed exercise sessions" (Methods, Participants). The authors themselves say the approach "may overestimate treatment efficacy". This meets the High anchor ("Per-protocol or as-treated analysis only, with exclusions or switching that could affect the result"). No intention-to-treat or modified analysis is offered.

**D3 Missing outcome data: High.** Of 13 randomized to RGBEG, 3 were left out of the analysis. That is 23% missing in one arm, above the anchor's 20%. The CG lost 2 of 12 (17%). "No imputation method was applied for missing data", and there is no sensitivity analysis. In the CG the reason given is "insufficient compliance with brace and exercise treatment". That is plausibly linked to the assigned programme, which strengthens the rating rather than weakening it.

**D4 Measurement of the outcome: Some concerns.** Low cannot be reached. Participants knew their arm, and the paper says that "participants' awareness of group allocation may have influenced responses to self-reported measures such as the WRVAS, MSPPA, and SUS" (Limitations). The instrument does meet the Some concerns conditions:
- It is published and validated (ref. 14).
- It is general to physical activity, with no game-specific items described.
- It was given at the same times in both arms: "Assessments were performed at T0 (baseline), T1 (12 weeks), and T2 (24 weeks)".

Nothing in the paper shows that items or administration differed between arms. The paper is silent on mode and on who administered it, and that silence does not meet the High anchor. One unresolved question belongs to extraction: the analysed total combines the motive subscales with an amotivation subscale, and the paper does not say whether amotivation is reverse-scored.

**D5 Selection of the reported result: High.**
- **Low fails.** The registration (NCT06469021, record date 16/06/2024) predates recruitment (14 October 2024). But its content is not in the saved text, so nothing shows that it names the MSPPA and a time point.
- **High is met on the subscale clause.** The Methods describe the administered instrument as having three subscales ("individual reasons, environmental reasons, and amotivation"). The Results give none of them, only the total. That fits the anchor's "gives only some of the subscales ... that its methods ... say were administered".
- **A second ground supports it.** There are three between-group analyses of the one outcome. The absolute T2 comparison is non-significant (p 0.13). The change-score and ANCOVA results are significant, and the ANCOVA is described as "additional" and not said to be pre-specified. The Abstract reports the ANCOVA. This is selection among analyses.
- **Why High rather than Some concerns.** A reader could argue that reporting the total is an analysis choice and not partial subscale reporting. On that reading D5 would be Some concerns. I take the more severe level because the subscales were collected and none is given.

## Direction re-examined, 2026-11-28 (amendment A11)
Every between-group test is on the MSPPA total, so the composite decides, and under A11(a) it decides only if the paper states how it is formed and its valence follows. The paper says only that the scale is "scored from 16 to 80" and "comprises three subscales: individual reasons, environmental reasons, and amotivation" (Methods, MSPPA); it never says whether the amotivation items are reverse-scored, so whether a higher total means more motivation does not follow from what is stated. The authors read it that way, but section 7 codes from the test and not from their reading. The code is therefore `other`, not `favours exergame`, and I have raised one query; the results stay recorded in `motivation_result`.
