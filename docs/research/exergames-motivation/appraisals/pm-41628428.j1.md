# Judgment — pm-41628428

- Records: pm-41628428
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The one open question was I2: the paper calls itself "quasi-experimental", and 7 participants were moved to the arm they asked for. Amendment A3 settles this. The paper says participants "were initially randomly allocated to either the EBTT+OCTT group or the OCTT×2 group as a parallel-group experimental study", and the switches were a minority (7 of 66) made at the participants' request: "These requests were made before commencement of the experimental procedure, and no participants were switched during the experimental period." That is A3 case (a), so I2 is met. The other criteria are plainly met:
- I1: Virtual Tennis on PlayStation 3 Move, a scored game played by swinging.
- I3: the comparator "practiced only on the traditional tennis court".
- I4: 12 weeks.
- I5: the Pelletier Sport Motivation Scale, seven 4-item subscales on motivation "for engaging in sports".
- I6: Table 3 gives pre and post results for both arms.
- I7: online 02.02.2026.
- I8: English.

## Reasons
**D1 High.** The paper names no method for generating the sequence and does not say allocation was concealed. Participants knew their assignment and used it: 7 asked to switch arms and "these requests were accommodated". So allocation was not concealed and was exploited. That is the High anchor, not just the silence that would give Some concerns. Baseline motivation looks similar in Table 3 (IM-to-know 24.18 vs 24.21), but that does not undo the switching. The authors themselves say it "introduced self-selection bias ... especially for outcomes related to motivation".

**D2 Some concerns.** The reported analysis does not keep participants in their randomized arms: it is "per protocol", by the group they "actually participated in". That rules out Low. High requires per-protocol or as-treated analysis *only*, and this paper also ran a modified ITT by assigned group, which it says gave consistent results ("generally consistent for all outcome measures"). That modified ITT table is in Multimedia Appendix 1, which is not in the saved text, so the claim cannot be checked here. The switches were small and similar in size (3 vs 4), they happened before the intervention, and no contamination or co-intervention is reported. So the High anchor is not met, and the domain falls to Some concerns.

**D3 Some concerns.** Dropout was 15% in the exergame arm and 12% in the comparator (Table 1). That is in the 10–20% band, with a gap between arms of about 3 points. The reasons are given only for the two arms together ("work schedules, health issues, transportation difficulties, or family emergencies"), and none is attributed to the intervention. Completers only were analysed, and the paper does not report the numbers analysed per arm. No arm is missing more than 20%, so not High. Missingness is over 10%, so not Low.

**D4 Some concerns.** The Sport Motivation Scale (Pelletier) is a published, validated instrument. It was given to both arms at the same two times ("pre- and post-12 weeks of training"), and nothing says items were changed or that administration differed. That meets the Some concerns conditions. Low is unattainable, because participants knew their arm and self-reported. One oddity has no rating consequence: the Methods call it the "Sports Motivation Test" and the Abstract the "Sport Motivation Scale". Both cite Pelletier and describe the same seven 4-item subscales.

**D5 Some concerns.** There is no registration or protocol ("This trial was not preregistered"), which rules out Low. Table 3 reports all seven subscales the Methods name, at both time points collected, so the High anchor (selective reporting of subscales or time points) does not apply.
