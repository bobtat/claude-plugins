## Coding notes
- `exergame_platform` = immersive vr headset. The paper never names the game or the headset. The only hardware statement is "After a short orientation on using VR headsets and safety procedures" (line 185). Chosen over `not stated` because a headset is mentioned; chosen over `other` because "custom software in a commercial headset" is the protocol's `immersive vr headset` case and nothing suggests purpose-built hardware.
- `comparator_type` = conventional exercise. The control arm is described only as "the control group (traditional physical activity without" (line 152) "VR)" (line 153). Chosen over `not stated` because "traditional physical activity" is exercise; chosen over `usual care` because nothing says the control continued usual activity. What the control did, how often and where is not described.
- `motivation_role` = exploratory or unspecified. The paper names exercise motivation and self-efficacy together as the two outcomes and never calls either primary or secondary: "focusing on exercise motivation and self-efficacy as dependent variables" (line 234).
- `trial_scale` = not stated. The paper does not call itself a pilot or feasibility trial; it calls itself "a randomized controlled trial".
- `setting` = not stated. Recruitment sites are named (community fitness centers and university campuses, line 148–149) but the paper does not say where sessions took place.
- `direction`: a choice between `favours exergame` and `not tested between groups`. The paper reports no between-group test at post-intervention alone. It reports a group main effect and a time x group interaction on one composite motivation score (Table 2), and its Bonferroni post-hoc tests (Table 3) are within-group over time. The composite decides under the protocol, and the time x group interaction is a between-group test on it, with the VR group's mean higher at both post-intervention and follow-up (Table 1), so `favours exergame`. Valence: the composite's construction is not described (see Domain evidence D4 and Inside the paper), so whether higher means more autonomous motivation cannot be checked in the paper; the paper itself reads a higher score as more motivation.
- `motivation_instrument` = breq: the paper names the BREQ-3.
- `population_group` = adults: eligibility bounds "between 18 and" 35 (lines 149–150).
- `health_status` = healthy or general population: "healthy adults" (Abstract, line 24), no condition recruited for.

### D4 Measurement of the outcome
Raising a concern:
- Instrument is named and described as validated: "The BREQ-3 has been validated in numerous populations, showing robust psychometric" (line 165).
- The results are one "Exercise Motivation" score per arm and time (Table 1), not the six BREQ-3 subscales. How a single score was formed from six subscales (some of which, amotivation, external and introjected, are not in the same direction as the others) is not stated; searched Data Collection (159–174), Data Analysis (232–243) and Results.
- The reported means do not sit within the scale as described: the items run "ranging from 0" (line 164) upward, 5 points, and the post-intervention VR mean is "4.08 (0.52)" (Table 1). See Inside the paper.
- Administration (mode, timing, who administered, referent wording) is not described, for either arm. Searched Data Collection.
- Participants' awareness of hypothesis or arm: not stated. The authors note: "Third, reliance on self-report measures for motivation and self-efficacy" (line 546) and "participants may have been influenced by social desirability or novelty effects associated with VR" (line 547).
- The Intervention text states the programme aimed at the outcome: "This eight-session VR exergame intervention was designed to systematically enhance exercise motivation" (line 176).
- Same instrument, same three time points for both arms: "followup assessments conducted at baseline, post-intervention, and at five months" (line 153); Table 1 gives both arms at all three times.
- No item changes are reported; none are said to have been made.

## Inside the paper
- Composite score above the scale's stated top: the instrument is described as 0 to 4 ("ranging from 0", line 164), yet the VR post-intervention mean is "4.08 (0.52)" (Table 1, motivation row), a mean above the maximum, and the text says it "increased substantially in the VR group (M = 4.08, SD = 0.52)" (line 283). A mean cannot exceed the maximum; either the scale, the composite (e.g. a sum or a different scoring) or the table is not as described. The paper does not say how the six subscales became one score.
- Self-efficacy is reported on a different range ("4-point Likert scale ranging from 1", line 171), and its means (3.94, 3.81) are within range; only the motivation score exceeds its range.
- Denominator: 30 randomized (15 + 15) but the group test is "F(1,27) = 21.77" (line 380) and Table 2 error df is 54 (the same error df as the time x group test, F(2,54)); with two groups and 30 participants a between-subjects test would carry 28 error df. No loss of participants is reported.
- Table 2: the time x group effect for motivation is reported as "η² = .45" (line 381; Table 2 ".45"). Using Table 2's own rows (SS 6.22 and error SS 8.28) a partial eta squared would be 6.22/(6.22+8.28) = .43 (my arithmetic, not the paper's). The group effect (SS 5.16, "η² = .43") similarly does not reproduce from the error row shown. Table 2 shows one error row per outcome only.
- Table 1 is cited for demographics ("The demographic characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1.", line 245) but Table 1 holds means and SDs of motivation and self-efficacy ("Table 1. Descriptive statistics (Means and Standard Deviations) for Exercise Motivation and Self-Efficacy", line 251). Demographics appear only in prose.
- The Abstract gives participant ages "(aged 19–33 years)" (line 25) against eligibility 18 to 35 (lines 149–150); consistent, not a conflict.
- Table 3 and Table 1 agree: VR motivation 4.08 − 2.91 = 1.17 ("1.17"), 3.92 − 2.91 = 1.01 ("1.01"), 3.92 − 4.08 = −0.16 ("-0.16"); control 3.15 − 2.87 = 0.28 ("0.28"), 3.12 − 2.87 = 0.25 ("0.25"). No disagreement found there.
- The Discussion says "Bonferroni post-hoc tests confirmed" (line 476) "that the VR group maintained higher scores across both dependent variables over time." (line 477), but Table 3 contains only within-group comparisons across time; no between-group post-hoc comparison at any time point is reported. The statement that the VR group "reported" "significantly higher gains in both motivation and self-efficacy compared to those in the control group" (line 468) rests on the time x group interaction and the within-group contrasts.
- The Discussion draws on participant feedback ("many reporting that leaderboards and performance", line 493) that has no corresponding method or data in the Methods or Results.
- The Introduction says the gap is "a lack of rigorous randomized controlled trials that compare VR exergames to traditional" exercise (line 141–142 text); the control arm here is "traditional physical activity" but is not described.
- Timing: the intervention "lasted eight structured sessions over two months" (line 153) and follow-up was "at five months" (line 153); whether five months counts from baseline or from the end of the programme is not stated.
- Exergame status: the paper never names the game or the headset, never says what the player does as game input. What it does say about play: "emphasizing coordination, movement accuracy, and responsiveness" (line 190); "higher-intensity VR exergame tasks that require endurance and focus" (line 206); "incorporating cooperative VR exergames" (line 211); "game-based motivational features such as levels, points, and rewards" (line 200). Body movement as the input is never stated; searched Introduction, Intervention, Discussion.

## Stated disagreements
The paper names prior work it differs from or qualifies, in its Discussion (lines 527–536):
- "Despite overall consistency with prior literature, some nuances should be noted." (line 527), then "other studies have highlighted variability depending on competitiveness and gamer profiles" (line 528), citing reference 26 (Lange-Nawka et al., 2024); the authors say individual differences are "a factor not directly addressed in our trial" (line 529 text).
- "research comparing immersive and non-immersive" (line 530) VR is said to suggest that both can increase activity (reference 21, Sheehy et al.), not a contradiction of the paper's result.
- "Some evidence suggests that VR may encourage moderate rather than" (line 533) high-intensity exertion (reference 31); the paper says physiological outcomes were not measured so this is left open.
