## Coding notes
- design = parallel rct: the paper calls itself "quasi-experimental" but says participants "were initially randomly allocated to either the EBTT+OCTT group or the OCTT×2 group as a parallel-group experimental study" (Methods, Study and Trial Designs).
- trial_scale = not stated: the paper never calls itself pilot or feasibility; it does call the study "exploratory" in the sample size paragraph ("as this study was exploratory"), which is not a listed value. Chosen between `not stated` and `full-scale`.
- setting = not stated: on-court sessions were "conducted on tennis courts"; where the exergame sessions took place is not given (only "requiring only about 4×3 meters of training space").
- exergame_platform = console or camera motion game: "Virtual Tennis (Sports Champion 2, Zindagi Games, San Diego Studio) on a gaming device, the PlayStation 3 Move" (Methods, Experimental Procedure).
- comparator_type = conventional exercise: the comparator arm "practiced only on the traditional tennis court" (Methods, Experimental Procedure).
- motivation_instrument = other: the paper names "Sports Motivation Test (SMT)" and "Sport Motivation Scale" (Abstract), "developed by Pelletier et al", 7 subscales of 4 items. It is not the Situational Motivation Scale (`sims`), BREQ or IMI and not a need-satisfaction scale.
- motivation_role = exploratory or unspecified: the paper lists motivation among outcomes ("Outcomes included ... motivation (using the Sport Motivation Scale)") and names none primary; it says "this study was exploratory".
- direction = mixed across subscales: no composite is reported. Between-group tests at end: IM-to-know P<.001 and IM-to-accomplish P<.001, with the exergame arm higher at post (22.39 vs 17.21; 21.82 vs 18.41), favour exergame; EM-external regulation P<.001 with the exergame arm higher at post (23.11 vs 18.24); external regulation lower is favourable, so this favours the comparator. Opposite arms on subscales with a valence, so `mixed across subscales`. The rest (IM-to-experience, EM-identified, EM-introjected, amotivation) not significant. The decision between this and `favours exergame` rests on reading higher external regulation as unfavourable.
- The between-group P in Table 3 is the group × time interaction in a repeated-measures ANOVA (Data Analysis); the table prints one P per outcome.

## Domain evidence

### D1 Randomization process
Raises concern:
- "participants were initially randomly allocated to either the EBTT+OCTT group or the OCTT×2 group as a parallel-group experimental study (aged 18 to 40 years, with balanced randomization in a ratio of 1:1)" (Methods, Study and Trial Designs). No sequence generation method named and no allocation concealment described; searched Methods, Experimental Procedure, Limitations.
- "about 9%-10% (OCTT×2 [n=3] and EBTT+OCTT [n=4]) of participants in each group requested to change their assigned allocation, and these requests were accommodated to minimize participant dropout. These requests were made before commencement of the experimental procedure, and no participants were switched during the experimental period." (Methods, Study and Trial Designs)
- "7 participants requested to join the group of their preference ... This introduced self-selection bias and could potentially affect the internal validity of the study’s results, especially for outcomes related to motivation." (Limitations)
- "findings about motivation in this study should be interpreted with caution" (Limitations)
Answers a concern:
- "Independent t tests revealed no significant differences between groups in age, training experience, sex (male and female), BMI, and physical activity levels ( Table 1 )." (Results, Overview)
- Baseline motivation (Table 3 pretest, EBTT+OCTT vs OCTT×2): "24.18 (2.92)" vs "24.21 (2.53)" IM-to-know; "23.29 (2.57)" vs "24.38 (2.21)" IM-to-accomplish; "24.54 (2.83)" vs "24.34 (2.79)" EM-external regulation. No test of baseline motivation is reported.
- "Although participants who had requested the group change may have had pre-existing differences in motivation, their baseline levels remained similar compared to those who remained in their allocated groups." (Limitations); no data for that comparison found in the saved text.
- Recruitment before allocation: "Social media postings and word-of-mouth marketing were used to recruit research participants." Not a cluster trial.

### D2 Deviations from intended interventions
- "Final analysis was conducted per protocol outlined in the “Data Analysis” section, where participants were analyzed according to the group they actually participated in, instead of their original randomized groups." (Methods, Data Analysis)
- "In addition, a modified intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was also performed, in which all participants who were randomized initially and completed the study were included in the analysis according to their originally assigned groups." (Methods, Data Analysis)
- "the treatment effect estimates from the modified ITT and per-protocol analyses were generally consistent for all outcome measures ... The table on the modified ITT analysis is provided in Multimedia Appendix 1 ." (Results, Overview). The appendix is titled "Results before group reassignment for modified intention-to-treat analysis" (line 363) and is not in the saved text. Tables 2 and 3 are presented without saying which analysis they use.
- "the per-protocol analysis of data reflected the partial allocation of participants’ chosen group, as opposed to randomized groups" (Limitations)
- "the consistency in results between the modified ITT and per-protocol analyses strengthens the validity of the findings" (Limitations)
- Co-interventions, contamination, extra sessions: nothing reported; searched Methods, Results, Limitations. Both groups "followed identical protocols for their on-court training sessions". Participants "were allowed to retain access to the tennis training facilities during the study period at no cost" (Ethical Considerations). The exergame console use outside sessions is not discussed.
- Participant blinding: "future studies should incorporate strategies such as ... participant blinding" (Limitations); participants knew their arm.

### D3 Missing outcome data
- "Four individuals in the OCTT×2 group and 5 in the EBTT+OCTT group dropped out during the second and third weeks, respectively" (Methods, Experimental Procedure); Table 1: "Dropouts, n (%) 5 (15) 4 (12)".
- "4 participants from the OCTT×2 group and 5 from the EBTT+OCTT group dropped out due to work schedules, health issues, transportation difficulties, or family emergencies" (Results, Overview); reasons not given per arm.
- "The data of participants who dropped out were not used in the analysis." (Results, Overview)
- Numbers analysed are not tabulated; the text implies 28 and 29: "28 of 33 participants in the EBTT+OCTT group", "29 of 33 participants in the OCTT×2 group" (Results, Effect on Motivation Level). Those phrases are attached to a change-in-score count, so whether they are the analysed n is an inference.
- "This analytical approach excludes missing data from participants who dropped out" (Methods, Data Analysis). No imputation.
- Missingness attributed to the intervention: none stated (reasons are listed as above).
- "The dropout rate was approximately 12% in the OCTT×2 group and 15% in the EBTT+OCTT group" (Limitations).

### D4 Measurement of the outcome
- "The Sports Motivation Test (SMT) and the Sports Confidence Inventory (SCI) were used to measure motivation and confidence levels of participants when playing sports pre- and post-12 weeks of training." (Methods)
- "The SMT, developed by Pelletier et al [ 34 ], is a self-report questionnaire that assessed 7 types or subscales of motivation" and "Each subscale score was calculated as the sum of all 4 items belonging to that subscale, resulting in 7 separate subscale scores ranging from 4 to 28." (Methods)
- "High internal consistency is indicated by Cronbach alpha coefficients ranging from 0.70 to 0.90" and "All questionnaires and outcome measures were valid and reliable" (Methods). No validation in this sample is reported.
- Items refer to "their motivation for engaging in sports", not to the exergame; same items stated for both arms. No change to items, language of administration, mode, timing within the session or administrator is described; searched Methods, Outcome Measures. Pre and post times: "pre- and post-12 weeks of training".
- Participant awareness of hypothesis: not stated. Assessor blinding (for tennis skill tests only): "While scoring the participant, the assessor was aware of the participant’s group allocation."
- Abstract names it "Sport Motivation Scale", Methods "Sports Motivation Test"; same instrument by the Pelletier citation.

### D5 Selection of the reported result
- "This trial was not preregistered, as it did not measure any clinical outcomes." (Methods, Ethical Considerations). No protocol cited; searched Methods, Funding, Data Availability.
- All 7 SMT subscales are reported at pre and post for both arms in Table 3; the Methods name the same 7 subscales. Only pre and post time points were collected: "pre- and post-12 weeks of training".
- Analysis plan stated in Methods, Data Analysis (repeated-measures ANOVA, post hoc tests). No other analyses of motivation are listed that go unreported.
- Data availability: "Study data can be provided upon reasonable request to the corresponding author."

## Inside the paper
- Abstract gives motivation figures that Table 3 does not: "IM-to-know (MD=7, SD 2.95) and IM-to-accomplish (MD=5, SD 3.77)". Table 3 gives "–7 (–8.43 to 5.57; 28.91)" and "–5.97 (–7.09 to 4.85; 24.49)"; the SDs 2.95 and 3.77 appear nowhere in the tables, and 5 differs from 5.97.
- Abstract says EBTT+OCTT "retained intrinsic motivation (IM) better", but Table 3 shows within-arm declines in the exergame arm too: IM-to-know "–1.79 (–3.85 to 0.28; 7.40) .04" and IM-to-accomplish "–1.46 (–2.90 to –0.03; 6.27) .04". The first has a 95% CI that includes zero beside P=.04.
- Results text: "28 of 33 participants in the EBTT+OCTT group showed a significant decrease in IM-to-accomplish, EM-identified, and EM-introjected"; IM-to-know in the same arm is also marked .04 in Table 3 and is not named. The Discussion lists the arm's significant decreases as "only in IM-to-accomplish (by 0.09 times), EM-identified (by 0.22 times), and EM-introjected (by 0.15 times)" and elsewhere "significant decreases ( P <.001) in EM-identified and EM-introjected", whereas Table 3 gives EM-introjected P ".01" for that arm.
- Effect size wording: the Methods define "medium (0.06-0.14), or large (≥0.14)", yet the Results say "A large effect size was also found, ηp² (IM-to-know)=0.25, and ηp² (IM-to-accomplish)=0.12". 0.12 is medium by the paper's definition.
- Table 3 within-group rows: amotivation EBTT+OCTT "7.08 (6.31 to 9.04; 51.75) .06" against OCTT×2 "7.79 (6.20 to 9.38; 56.20) <.001" with near-equal mean changes; the CI for 7.08 is not centred on it. CIs "(–8.43 to 5.57" and "(–7.09 to 4.85" lack minus signs.
- Table 3 prints one between-group P per outcome but not the F, df or effect size; the effect sizes are only in the text.
- Hit-score fold change: Results "hit score increased by 5 times (MD 17.14)" vs Discussion "hit scores increased by 5.5 times".
- Reassignment: Methods "about 9%-10% (OCTT×2 [n=3] and EBTT+OCTT [n=4])"; 4 of 33 is about 12%.
- Design label: the title and Conclusion say "quasi-experimental" while the Abstract says "66 novices were randomized"; the reassignment and "per protocol" analysis by the group actually joined are described in Methods and Limitations.
- Table 2 grip strength posttest SDs "0.82" and "0.68" against pretest SDs "14.96" and "13.97"; confidence posttest SD "2.90" vs pretest "9.21".
- Discussion: "Although overall motivation decreased slightly over the 12 weeks, the decline was smaller in EBTT+OCTT" vs "While the effect sizes in this quasi-experimental study were large in terms of motivational outcomes".
- Discussion "Significant decreases were noted in IM-to-know (by 0.28 times), IM-to-accomplish (by 0.24 times), EM-identified (by 0.20 times), and EM–external regulation (by 0.25 times)" for OCTT×2; the Results text also lists EM-introjected for that arm (Table 3 ".02").

## Stated disagreements
- "Notably, there was a study that found exergaming to be less effective than traditional training for psychological outcomes. The trial reported that while exergaming boosted enjoyment and engagement, it did not significantly improve confidence in novice athletes [ 52 ]. The difference may reflect variations in exergame type, intervention length, or participant skill level." (Discussion, Effect of Exergaming on Confidence Level)
- "The substantially greater improvements observed in this quasi-experimental study may be attributable to the longer intervention duration (12 weeks vs 2 weeks)" (Discussion, grip strength, against an electromyography biofeedback exergaming study [ 40 ]).
- "The greater improvement observed in this study (average skill increase of 5.5 times) compared with previous work (around 1.3 times) may be explained by the novice status of participants." (Discussion, Effect of Exergaming on Technical Skills)
