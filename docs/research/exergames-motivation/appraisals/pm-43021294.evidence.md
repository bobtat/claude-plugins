## Coding notes
- `setting` = `other`: sessions took place in a clinic setting, online by video call, and at home. "In both groups, one clinic-based session and one online session (via WhatsApp video calls) were conducted each week" and "the home exercise program was performed 3 days per week for 45–55 min" (Methods, Interventions). The Methods say the study was run at "the Aktif R&D Academy Consulting Center".
- `exergame_platform` = `other`: the game system spans two kinds of hardware. Clinic: "BeCure Balance system using a balance board and laptop camera" (Appendix 2); home and online: "BeCure Smart Pose application using the front camera of a smartphone or tablet" (Appendix 2). The list values `custom rehabilitation system` (purpose-built hardware), `console or camera motion game` (custom software on a commercial camera) and `mobile or ar` each fit one part and not the other.
- `motivation_instrument` = `other`: the instrument is the Physical Activity Participation Motivation Scale (MSPPA), not on the list ("a validated 16-item, 5-point Likert-type scale ... that measures motivation to participate in physical activity").
- `motivation_role` = `secondary`: "Secondary outcomes included trunk rotation, motivation to participate in physical activity, balance, and system usability" (Abstract); also under "Secondary outcome measures" heading (line 84–88).
- `direction` = `favours exergame`, a choice between list values. The paper gives three between-group tests on the MSPPA total at the end of the intervention (T2, 24 weeks), and they do not agree on significance: absolute T2 scores "0.13 b"; change scores "< 0.001 a" (Mann–Whitney U); baseline-adjusted ANCOVA "F(1,17) = 7.37, p = 0.015". The absolute-score text says "no significant between-group differences were observed in the absolute MSPPA scores at T0, T1, or T2 ( p  > 0.05)". The composite total (not subscales) is what is tested. I coded from the change-score and ANCOVA tests, in the direction of higher scores in the exergame arm (the paper reads a smaller decline as favourable). The alternative would be `no between-group difference` on absolute T2. The paper does not say whether higher totals mean more motivation given that one of the three subscales is amotivation; the paper reads the totals as higher = more motivation ("Motivation levels significantly declined in the control group").
- `comparator_type` = `conventional exercise`: "the CG continued with conventional exercises" (Abstract/Methods); both arms also received bracing and PSSE.
- `population_group` = `children and adolescents`: "age 10–14 years" (Inclusion criteria). `health_status` = `musculoskeletal`: adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (spinal deformity).
- `country` is not stated as a sentence; taken from the corresponding affiliation "Istanbul, Fatih Turkey" and ethics committee "Istanbul Medipol University".

## Domain evidence

### D1 Randomization process
Raises or leaves a question:
- No named sequence method beyond a web tool: "the random allocation sequence was created using" a website, see next line; no block size or stratification stated (searched Methods, Sample size, randomization, and blinding).
- Baseline motivation differs numerically: "33.70 ± 7.90" (CG) against "27.84 ± 2.32" (RGBEG), Table 4 T0 row, with "0.12 b" and effect size "0.72". Text: "At baseline, no significant difference in total MSPPA scores was observed between the CG and the RGBEG".
- Unequal arms: "12 participants were allocated to the CG and 13 to the RGBEG" (Results) under a stated 1:1 ratio ("in a 1:1 ratio", Study design). No block size stated.
- Baseline Table 1 thoracic Cobb "18.50 ± 16.16 (0–36)" and "23.70 ± 10.61 (0–40)"; lumbar "16.90 ± 12.62" and "23.20 ± 10.42"; all p > 0.05. Baseline MSPPA is not in Table 1.
- Participants who were allocated after baseline assessment: "After completion of the baseline assessment, the principal investigator opened the sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes".

Answers:
- "by an independent staff member who was not involved in participant enrolment, assessment, intervention delivery, or data analysis".
- Concealment: "sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes".
- "Eligibility screening and participant enrolment were performed by a physician experienced in scoliosis care."
- "There were no statistically significant between-group differences in demographic or clinical characteristics at baseline".

### D2 Deviations from intended interventions
Raises:
- Analysis population: "Analyses were conducted according to a per-protocol approach and included participants who completed the 24-week intervention and follow-up assessments."
- "Participants who did not demonstrate adequate adherence to the treatment protocol after randomization were not included in the final analyses."
- Exclusion rule: "attended fewer than 80% of the prescribed exercise sessions were not included in the per-protocol analysis".
- Authors' own caution: "this approach may overestimate treatment efficacy, as participants with poor adherence or incomplete follow-up were excluded".
- Authors' recommendation: "should employ an intention-to-treat analysis" (Discussion).
- Participants and therapist knew the arm: "blinding of the participants and the physiotherapist delivering the intervention to group allocation was not possible".
- Game arm's programme mixed: the first 4 weeks were conventional in both arms ("the first 4 weeks of the program were administered using the same procedure in both groups"); game used from week 5.
- "not all exercises could be fully implemented through the digital platforms" (Limitations).

Answers:
- "The planned exercise frequency and session duration were matched between groups."
- "participants in the CG were instructed to refrain from any additional exercise or rehabilitation intervention outside the study protocol".
- Exclusion of competing exercise: "participation in any other structured exercise program".
- No contamination, switching, or crossover of CG participants to games is reported; searched Results and Limitations. Not a crossover trial.

### D4 Measurement of the outcome
- Instrument: "Physical Activity Participation Motivation Scale (MSPPA)"; "a validated 16-item, 5-point Likert-type scale scored from 16 to 80 that measures motivation to participate in physical activity and comprises three subscales: individual reasons, environmental reasons, and amotivation". Validation reference 14: "Physical Activity Participation Motivation Scale (PAPMS): a validity and reliability study".
- Timing: "Assessments were performed at T0 (baseline), T1 (12 weeks), and T2 (24 weeks)." Same schedule stated for all outcomes; no arm difference in MSPPA administration is stated. Who administered it, the mode (paper or screen) and whether items were adapted are not stated (searched Outcome measures, Statistical analysis, Limitations).
- Items identical across arms: not explicitly stated; the scale is general to physical activity, with no game-specific items described.
- Awareness: "participants' awareness of group allocation may have influenced responses to self-reported measures such as the WRVAS, MSPPA, and SUS". Whether participants were told the hypothesis is not stated.
- The assessor-blinding in the Abstract applies to the radiological assessment and the statistician: "the physician who performed the radiological assessments and the statistician who conducted the statistical analyses were blinded to group allocation".
- The Results report the MSPPA total only; subscale scores are not reported in any table (see D5).

### D5 Selection of the reported result
- "ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT06469021 (Record date: 16/06/2024)" (Abstract). "Participant recruitment began on 14 October 2024" (Study setting): the record date precedes recruitment by the dates given. The registry entry itself is not in the saved text, so what it names as outcomes, instrument and time point is not known.
- Methods list the instrument with three subscales ("individual reasons, environmental reasons, and amotivation"); Results give "total MSPPA scores" and Table 4 only a total row. No subscale results are reported or said to be omitted.
- Multiple analyses of the one outcome: absolute scores, change scores, and "additional ANCOVA models were performed for the MSPPA outcome" ("To adjust for potential baseline variability and obtain a more precise estimate of the intervention effect"); the ANCOVA is described as additional, and no statement says it was pre-specified.
- Abstract reports the ANCOVA results ("p = 0.207", "p = 0.015") and not the absolute-score tests at T1 and T2.
- MSPPA is among secondary outcomes; the primary outcomes were Cobb angle, POTSI and WRVAS.

## What the appendices say on the screening doubt (I1)
Reported as text, with no ruling.
- Clinic games: "BeCure Balance system using a balance board and laptop camera"; "Participants shifted their body weight while maintaining the prescribed posture to move the on-screen balance board and guide a ball toward the target locations" (Appendix 2, Balance Maze); feedback "Real-time visual feedback, target achievement, score, and task duration".
- Balance Surf: "Participants controlled an on-screen surfboard through weight shifting and postural adjustments to reach reward zones on a moving water surface"; feedback "Real-time visual feedback, reward zones, score, and task duration".
- Home games: "Participants moved an on-screen avatar using predefined unilateral or bilateral upper-extremity and body positions to avoid obstacles and increase their score" (Runner); "Participants positioned their body and extremities to move an on-screen avatar through approaching gates at the correct time and position" (Gates), with "successful gate passage, and score".
- Mixed programme: "The first 4 weeks were identical in both groups and did not include game-based exercises. The game-based intervention was implemented from weeks 5 to 24." (Appendix 2, Note). Arms also share bracing and PSSE (Appendix 1).
- Design differences between the arms limited to delivery: "the groups differed only in the delivery of core stabilization and balance exercises" (Appendix 1, note).

## Inside the paper
- Table 4, RGBEG change scores do not follow from its means. Means "27.84 ± 2.32" (T0), "27.75 ± 2.60" (T1), "29.60 ± 3.98" (T2) give changes of about −0.09 (T0–T1) and +1.76 (T0–T2) on the same n = 10, but the change rows show "0.10 ± 2.51" and "0.10 ± 4.12" (both +0.10). For the CG the means do reproduce the changes ("−7.10", "−8.70").
- Absolute T2 scores do not differ ("0.13 b"), yet the change score does ("< 0.001 a") and so does the ANCOVA ("p = 0.015"). The baseline imbalance (33.70 vs 27.84, Table 4) lies between these readings; the text calls the baseline difference non-significant ("p = 0.12").
- Table 4 CG T0 MSPPA 33.70 on a 16–80 scale falls to "25.00" at T2; RGBEG sits at about 28–30. Arithmetic regression to the mean toward a common level (CG 33.70 → 25.00, RGBEG 27.84 → 29.60, a crossing) is a possible reading; the paper does not discuss it.
- Abstract and Conclusion describe motivation as "maintained" in the game arm; Table 4's RGBEG within-group p is "0.65" (T0–T2) and the Friedman p is "0.83 d".
- Study is labelled 1:1 yet allocation was 12 and 13 (stated in Results; no block size given).
- Inclusion requires "a Cobb angle of 25°–40°" but Table 1 lists thoracic Cobb minima of "(0–36)" and "(0–40)", lumbar "(0–33)", "(0–37)": these are single-region curves, so the minima of 0 are not necessarily at odds, but the paper does not say which region the inclusion Cobb applies to.
- Appendix 1 note names the intervention group "ROTEG" while the main text names it "RGBEG".
- Discussion: "adherence was not objectively quantified using comparable measures in both groups", against Methods: "In both groups, brace and exercise adherence was monitored using a Brace and Exercise Diary". No adherence figures are reported in Results.
- Discussion states "The favorable radiological course observed in our study suggests that the feedback mechanisms provided by digital systems ... may be relevant", while its own between-group tests for Cobb angle show no difference ("p = 0.46", "p = 0.58", "p = 0.26").
- Results text says the lumbar between-group p is 0.58 and thoracolumbar 0.26 in that order ("p = 0.46, p = 0.58 and p = 0.26, respectively" for thoracic, lumbar, thoracolumbar); Table 2 lists TL "0.26 a" and L "0.58 b", consistent.

## Stated disagreements
- "However, a study of individuals with AIS performing exergame-based exercise at home reported that interest in the games and exercise frequency declined over time [ 20 ]." (Discussion; Wibmer et al. [20]; the paper's own motivation did not decline in the game arm.)
- "The discrepancy between that finding and our results may be related to the limited sample size inherent in our pilot design" (Discussion; Yuan et al. [19], who found more pronounced Cobb improvement in the digital care group).
- "In contrast, Xuan et al. [ 25 ] and Şahin et al. [ 26 ] reported reduced static balance ability." (Discussion, on balance in scoliosis; opposite to Meng, Xu and Piątek-Krzywicka.)
