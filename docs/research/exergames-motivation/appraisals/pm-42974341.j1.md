# Judgment — pm-42974341

- Records: pm-42974341
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The study meets I2 by amendment A3 and no further. The paper calls itself "quasi-experimental" and follows TREND for non-randomized designs (line 42). It also says "Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups" and that allocation used "a pseudo-randomized procedure constrained to produce approximately equal group sizes" (line 45). A3 names exactly this case, a paper that describes allocation as random but made by a constrained or pseudo-randomized procedure, as in. The procedure itself is never described. It could have been alternation, which I2 puts out, but the text does not show that, so A3's wording governs. The weakness is rated under D1.

I6, the doubt screening recorded: the IMI went to the Wii Fit and wobble-board groups only ("participants in the two training groups (gamified and traditional) completed the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory", line 64). Results are given for both groups in Table 2. Under A8, results for the exergame arm and any one non-exergame arm are enough, so I6 is met.

The other criteria are plainly met:
- I1: Wii Fit Plus balance games played by weight shifts (line 71).
- I3: wobble board and no-training control.
- I4: four weeks (line 70).
- I5: IMI.
- I7: published November 2026.
- I8: English.

## Reasons
**D1 — High.** One investigator enrolled participants, generated the allocation and assigned groups, and "allocation was not concealed" (line 45). No sequence method is named; the only die mentioned sets the test order (line 49). That is allocation that was not concealed in a way that could be exploited: the person enrolling each athlete knew the next assignment and could steer who went where. It is not Some concerns, because concealment is not just unreported here; the paper says it was absent. Baseline motivation cannot be checked either, since the IMI was given post-training only.

**D2 — Some concerns.** Three participants who were injured in sport "discontinued before completing the protocol and were not included in the final analysis" (line 67). The analysis is of completers. Since those three seem to have had no outcome data, it could pass as a modified ITT. But the analysis population for the IMI is not clearly described: Table 2 is headed "Gamified N=12" while the text says 9 were analysed. Usual team practice continued in every arm (line 155), which is a co-intervention balanced between arms. That fits Some concerns. No contamination or unbalanced deviation is reported, so not High.

**D3 — High.** 12 were allocated to the gamified arm (line 45) and 9 analysed (line 78). That is 25% missing in that arm, against 0% in the traditional arm (11 of 11). Both High triggers apply: over 20% missing in one arm, and a gap between arms of more than 10 percentage points. No robustness analysis is reported. The reason given, sport injury, is not attributed to the intervention, but that does not lift the domain out of High. Table 2's "N=12" contradicts the text's n = 9 rather than resolving it.

**D4 — Some concerns.** The IMI is a published, validated instrument. It was given at the same time, after post-testing, to both arms whose results are compared. Participants knew their arm, so Low is not attainable. Nothing shows a High condition: the items are in a supplement that was not saved, so a rewrite or game-specific items cannot be shown. The validation citation (Ryan & Deci 2000) is a general paper on self-determination theory, and who administered the IMI is not stated, but neither is a High condition under the anchor.

**D5 — Some concerns.** No registration or protocol is cited anywhere. The paper reports to TREND (line 42). That is the Some concerns anchor's "no registration or protocol" case. All four subscales the Methods say were assessed (line 64) are reported in Table 2 at the one stated time point, so the High anchor (only some subscales or time points reported) does not apply.
