# Judgment — pm-40567995

- Records: pm-40567995
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
I1: the Kinect system scores limb angles against a model ("If a participant's limb angles were within 5° of the standards, they earned points.", §2.4) and "The awarding score would display on the screen as an incentive for the participants" (§2.4). Amendment A4 covers this case directly: a movement-training system that keeps a running score for matching target movements. I2 is met by computer-generated block randomization (§2.3). I3 is met by a video-only aerobic dance control with "no performance score for feedback as incentives" (§2.4). I4 is met: "three times a week for 8 weeks" (§2.4). I5 is met by the BREQ-2, Chinese version (§2.5, §2.5.4). I6 is met: Table 2 gives BREQ-2 means and SDs for each arm at weeks 8 and 12, and means count under I6 whatever the column labels. That answers the screening doubt about the unlabelled between-group test: the ambiguity concerns how the result is coded (`direction`), not whether results by arm exist. I7 is met: published 2025, with no earlier results report cited. I8 is met: English.

## Reasons
**D1 Some concerns.** A random sequence method is named (computer-generated, blocks of 4, allocated by independent researchers), but the paper never says that allocation was concealed until assignment, so the Low anchor is not met. Baseline BREQ-2 differs (EG 47.57 ± 6.75 against CG 53.97 ± 17.22) and is not tested. With a CG SD that large, this does not clearly show a failed randomization, so the High anchor is not met either.

**D2 Some concerns.** The paper makes no ITT or per-protocol statement. 65 were randomized by §3.1 but 60 by the Abstract, and only the 60 who "finished the training session and all the measurements" are analysed. The paper does not say whether the five excluded had partial outcome data. The analysis population is therefore not adequately described, which is a Some concerns case. No contamination, switching or unbalanced deviation is reported, so the domain is not High. The differing exercise environments (§5) are part of the interventions as designed, not deviations from them.

**D3 Some concerns.** 5 of 65 (about 8%) have no outcome data. The paper does not say which arm they came from, how many were randomized to each arm, or why they left (the Figure 1 content is not in the text). So the paper cannot show 90% or more retained in each arm, and Low is not met. Nothing attributes missingness to the intervention, and no arm is shown to have lost more than 20%, so High is not met.

**D4 Some concerns.** The BREQ-2 is a published instrument, used in a validated Chinese translation (ref. 22), self-completed "before the walking test" at the same three time points in both arms. That meets the Some concerns conditions, and no High condition (modified items, game-specific items, different administration) is reported. Low is not attainable. The paper's claim that participants "remained blinded" is implausible when only one arm saw a skeleton and a score. In any case the paper does not say participants were unaware of the hypothesis. How the single BREQ-2 score was formed is not stated, and that is noted here; the protocol's High list does not cover it.

**D5 High.** The Methods present the BREQ-2 as divided into five aspects (§2.5.4), yet Table 2 and §3.2 report only one unexplained BREQ-2 score per arm and no subscale anywhere. That meets the High anchor: "the report gives only some of the subscales ... that its methods ... say were administered". Separately, the registration NCT06216236 is cited without a date or a listed outcome or time point. On its own that would be Some concerns, and it cannot lift the domain to Low.
