# Judgment — pm-41307454

- Records: pm-41307454
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
I1: the VirZoom bike drives games with goals, one being a race through "15 timing gates" where "Participants aimed to meet various performance goals at each gate" (Intervention Group), so it is more than scenery feedback. I2: "Participants were randomly assigned to either the intervention group or the control group" (Study Design). I3: a "no-intervention control group" (Abstract). I4: "twice weekly, for 4 weeks" (Intervention Group). I5: the BREQ-2. I6: Table 2 gives all five subscales for both arms at 4 weeks. I7: published 15 December 2025, and the paper cites no earlier results report of this trial. I8: English. Every criterion is met.

## Reasons
**D1 Some concerns.** The sequence method is named ("computer-generated sequence in Microsoft Excel") and baseline BREQ-2 subscales are similar (Table 1, all P≥.17). The paper never says allocation was concealed: who generated the sequence and who assigned participants after the baseline assessments is not reported. The Low anchor requires concealment, so the rating is Some concerns. Nothing shows the allocation was predictable, so it is not High.

**D2 Some concerns.** The two dropouts have no outcome data, which on its own would be compatible with Low. But the paper says "any identified outliers were excluded from the analyses" without saying how many or from which outcomes, so the analysis population is not fully described. The control arm's activity was not tracked, and the authors say they cannot be sure the controls kept their usual routines. No switching, contamination or per-protocol-only analysis is reported, so it is not High.

**D3 High.** The paper reports 38 randomized, 2 dropouts both from the intervention arm, and 19 controls analysed. So 19 were randomized to each arm, and 17/19 (89.5%) of the intervention arm was analysed, against 19/19 of the controls. That fails Low's 90% per arm. The difference between arms, 10.5 percentage points, crosses High's "differing between arms by more than 10 percentage points", and the paper reports no robustness analysis. The dropout reason (COVID-era concern about in-person sessions) is not attributed to the intervention, but the High anchor is met on the between-arm difference alone. The margin is narrow, and the unknown number of outlier exclusions adds to the uncertainty rather than removing it.

**D4 Some concerns.** The BREQ-2 is a published, validated instrument. No item changes are described, and it was given the same way at baseline and 4 weeks in both arms ("Both groups received identical assessments at baseline and after intervention"). Participants knew their arm, and nothing says they were unaware of the hypothesis, so Low is out of reach. No High condition applies.

**D5 Some concerns.** The trial is registered, but the paper gives no registration date and does not say what outcomes, instruments or time points the registration names. Recruitment ran "between August 2020 and March 2021", and nothing shows the registration came before that. All five BREQ-2 subscales and the only two time points described in the Methods are reported (Table 2), so no High condition is shown. One thing to note: the reported statistics are internally inconsistent. The identified-regulation interaction repeats the main-effect statistics exactly, and F(1,34)=11.21 is printed with P=.02. This is a reporting-quality concern, not selective reporting as the anchors define it.
