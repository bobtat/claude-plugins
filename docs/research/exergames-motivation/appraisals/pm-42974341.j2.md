# Judgment — pm-42974341

- Records: pm-42974341
- Eligibility: clear

## Eligibility
The open criterion is I2. The paper calls itself "quasi-experimental" and follows TREND,
but it also says "Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups" and that
allocation used "a pseudo-randomized procedure constrained to produce approximately equal
group sizes" (Methods, Participants, line 45). That is the case amendment A3 places inside
I2: allocation the paper describes as random but made by a constrained or
pseudo-randomized procedure. The weakness is carried in D1. I6 was the screeners' other
doubt. The no-training control did not complete the IMI ("participants in the two
training groups (gamified and traditional) completed the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory",
line 64), but the wobble-board arm has IMI results (Table 2), and amendment A8 accepts
that. The other criteria are met. I1: Wii Fit Plus balance games played by shifting
weight. I3: wobble board and no-training arms. I4: "three times per week over four
weeks" (line 70). I5: the IMI. I7: published 2026. I8: English.

## Reasons

**D1 Randomization process: High.** The paper names no sequence method, only a
"pseudo-randomized procedure constrained to produce approximately equal group sizes".
The same investigator enrolled participants, generated the allocation and did all the
testing, and the paper says "allocation was not concealed" (line 45). Constrained
allocation that the enrolling person can see is predictable and open to exploitation,
which meets the High anchor. Baseline motivation cannot rescue it, because the IMI was
given only after training.

**D2 Deviations from intended interventions: Some concerns.** Analysis is of completers.
The three participants who were injured "discontinued before completing the protocol and
were not included in the final analysis" (line 67), so they appear to have no outcome
data. That would be a modified ITT, and their loss is a D3 matter. It falls short of Low
because the IMI analysis population is described inconsistently: Table 2 is headed
"Gamified N=12" while the text says n = 9 were analysed. Both arms also kept up team
practice, which is a co-intervention, though a balanced one. It does not meet High
because no switching, contamination or unbalanced deviation is reported.

**D3 Missing outcome data: High.** Allocation was gamified 12, traditional 11 and
control 10 (line 45). The analysed numbers were gamified 9, traditional 11 and control 10
(line 78). That leaves 25% of the exergame arm missing against 0% in the wobble-board
comparator. This exceeds both High thresholds: more than 20% missing in an arm, and a
difference of more than 10 percentage points between arms. No robust analysis is
offered. The missingness is attributed to sport injury, not to the intervention. Table
2's "N=12" makes the true IMI denominator uncertain, but it does not establish that the
data are complete.

**D4 Measurement of the outcome: Some concerns.** Low cannot be reached, because
participants knew their arm and rated their own motivation. The IMI is a published,
validated instrument, given at the same time (after post-testing) to both arms being
compared. Selecting four subscales is ordinary IMI use. Nothing in the text shows items
added, rewritten beyond the referent, or game-specific, or that administration differed
between arms. The item table is not in the saved text, so the referent cannot be
checked. That leaves the domain at Some concerns, not High.

**D5 Selection of the reported result: Some concerns.** The paper reports no
registration or protocol. The only reporting framework it cites is TREND (line 42). This
is the anchor's "no registration or protocol" case. It is not High, because all four
subscales that the Methods say were assessed are reported in Table 2, at the single time
point administered.
